Compare the sounds: A (in the app now) and B (the new palette)

Everything on this page plays through the palette chosen at the top. A is the app's engine as shipped (src/sound.js of v15). B is the proposal: struck and bowed materials, a new variation on every play, a speed-following slider sound. The two are matched in loudness cue by cue, so you can judge timbre rather than volume. Laptop speakers or headphones at a moderate level. Esc stops every sound.

Try the slider below in A, then in B. Then hover the plane with the pointer.

No sound on an iPhone or iPad? Turn off the silent switch (Web Audio follows it).

1. The Deformation (the slider that "cracks")

Drag t like in the app. A ticks every 0.05 of t. B breathes with how fast you drag, rings a wooden note at t = 0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1, and is silent when your hand stops. Press play to hear the start, the motion and the arrival. B's slider is about 4 dB quieter than A's by design.

2. Hear the function

Hover: the tone's pitch class is arg F, so circling a zero climbs. Near a zero or a pole it rings its order (white dots: zeros, black: poles). Press and draw a loop: a bell for each full turn of arg F, then the winding number when you let go. Rest the pointer: in B the tone starts to sing (vibrato); in A it holds still.

4. Every cue, one by one

A plays the same waveform every time. B is a new strike on every press: press it several times, or use ×4. Where pitch carries meaning (the turn bell, the order pings) only level and brightness vary. Here every press plays at full level; in the app, B softens a cue repeated within 2 s by up to 4 dB (A does not).
